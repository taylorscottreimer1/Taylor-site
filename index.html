Research

Authority is being redistributed. Most of us never noticed.

Taylor's research examines how Instagram's algorithmic infrastructure shapes religious and epistemic authority for women: who gets amplified, who gets trusted, and who quietly disappears from view. Religion is her case study; the question reaches every institution that depends on being believed.

Algorithmic authorityHow ranking systems decide whose knowledge circulates and whose stays local.
Gender and credibilityWhy women's expertise is weighed differently, online and in institutions.
Digital religionHow faith communities and their leaders are reshaped by platforms.
Media literacy and AI in learningPractical formation for students, teachers and adult learners.

About

Taylor Scott-Reimer is a communication scholar, lecturer and author. She holds an MSc in Communication from Purdue University and is a Certified Training and Development Professional. She teaches at Tyndale University and develops curriculum for Knox College at the University of Toronto.

A self-described Zillennial who grew up between an analog childhood and the early social media era, Taylor speaks with clarity and warmth to audiences navigating trust, formation and technology. She lives in Barrie, Ontario, with her husband and two young children.
